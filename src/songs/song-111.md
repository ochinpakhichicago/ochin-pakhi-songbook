[Glossary]
murshidi | মুর্শিদি | songs addressed to one's spiritual guide (murshid) — a devotional folk genre distinct from, but often sung alongside, Baul songs
sari gaan | সারি গান | boat-race songs, sung by rowers in rhythm with their strokes
ghatu gaan | ঘাটু গান | a now-rare folk theatre/song tradition of Sylhet and Mymensingh
gazir gaan | গাজির গান | narrative folk songs performed in the monsoon season in honor of Gazi Pir, a Sufi saint venerated by both Hindus and Muslims in rural Bengal
bhanita | ভণিতা | the lyricist's signature line near a song's end — here, "Korim dinohin kon pothe jaitam," where Shah Abdul Karim names himself

[Discussion.Summary]
A nostalgic lament for a vanished era of rural Hindu-Muslim communal life in Sylhet, remembered through the shared folk music that once bound the two communities together — Baul and Murshidi songs sung jointly, Jatra theatre at Hindu neighbors' homes, boat races accompanied by Sari songs, and Gazir Gaan performed each monsoon for a saint honored by both faiths. Against that remembered harmony, the song sets the narrator's bewildered, almost comic first encounter with modern politics ("someone was a 'member,' someone a 'minister' — did we even know these names?"), before closing on Karim's own signature line: aging, poor, and uncertain which way to turn as harder days keep arriving.
