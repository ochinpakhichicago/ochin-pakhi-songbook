[Refrain]
T: Pindare polasher bon
B: পিন্দারে পলাশের বন
E: In Pindar's palash forest,

T: Palabo palabo mon
B: পালাব পালাব মন
E: my heart wants to flee, to flee

[Verse 1]
T: (Hei) Nyangta indure dhol kate
B: (হেই) ন্যাংটা ইঁদুরে ঢোল কাটে

T: He kate re.
B: হে কাটে রে।
E: oh, it gnaws it,

T: (Are) Botore piriter phul phute.
B: (আরে) বটোরে পিরিতের ফুল ফুটে।
E: (ah) in the season of botor, the flower of love blooms

[Glossary]
Pindar | পিন্দার | likely a place name, associated with the tribal Jharkhand region where the song's roots and the film "Joram" are set
polasher bon | পলাশের বন | a forest of palash (Flame-of-the-forest) trees, blooming vivid red
palabo palabo mon | পালাব পালাব মন | the heart wanting to run away, to elope — a recurring folk theme of romantic escape
nyangta indur | ন্যাংটা ইঁদুর | a "naked," bare-tailed mouse — part of a whimsical rustic image of the mouse gnawing a drum
botor | বটোর | a dialect/seasonal term of uncertain precise meaning — likely referring to a time of year associated with courtship
piriter phul phota | পিরিতের ফুল ফোটা | the flower of love blooming — love awakening
ratkana | রাতকানা | night-blindness, a real folk-recognized condition, used here affectionately of the bride
dhan kuta | ধান কোটা | pounding paddy by hand, a traditional daily household task
alta, sindur | আলতা, সিঁদুর | a red dye applied to the feet, and vermillion applied to a married woman's hair-parting — both markers of marriage
biha | বিহা | wedding, marriage — a dialect form of "biye"
boch chura | বৌচ চূড়া | a hair-knot or topknot, a woman's hairstyle
badula | বাদুলা | likely an owl, considered an omen of ill fortune in folk belief; the line warns against bringing bad luck onto another's household

[Arrangement]
A Santal-region folk song by Sunil Mahato, popularized by Shilajit. Gained wider recognition after being featured, without the artists' knowledge, in the 2023 film "Joram" starring Manoj Bajpayee.
